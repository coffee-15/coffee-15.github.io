About

Our engineering, business and design teams work with industry leaders across Aotearoa New Zealand, from waste collectors and recyclers to processors and manufacturers.

Over 5 years, we have enjoyed industrial site visits, undertaken lab experiments, held countless interviews, hosted industry workshops, designed prototypes, and published papers

Together, we’re testing what it takes to make circular practices work across the plastics supply chain.

Project Elements

Engineering

Our engineers develop recycling technologies and new materials that give manufacturers more ways to use hard-to-recycle plastics.


One team develops plasma treatment that can be integrated into extrusion, helping plastics that are difficult to mix form more consistent blends. The other develops and tests high-performance composites made from recycled plastics, including materials strengthened with reinforcing fibres.


Four industry demonstrators explore how these advances could be applied in local manufacturing

Business

Recyclers need buyers for the plastics they recover. Manufacturers need a dependable supply of materials that meet their specifications. Our business researchers study how to make both sides of that market work together.


By conducting industry interviews, focus groups, literature reviews and international case studies, the business team maps how the sector works and identifies barriers to change.


It works with partners to develop circular business models and market strategies, identifying the suppliers, buyers and incentives needed to make new recycling pathways commercially viable

Design

We build digital maps, data models and interactive simulations that help industry partners see how their decisions affect the wider plastics system. By modelling system mechanics, partners can test what it would take to keep more plastic in use and decide which changes to pursue together.


Drawing on systems design, human factors research, data visualisation and interaction design, the team develops interactive maps, system simulators, and strategy development tools that trace plastics from manufacture through use to recovery.


The Material Flow Dashboard shows how plastics move through Aotearoa New Zealand, from sourcing and manufacture to use, recovery and disposal. In the Circular Analysis Tool, users model a product’s current life cycle, identify where material is lost or where recovery breaks down, and test changes from a research-backed database of interventions. They can try a new recycling process, a take-back scheme or a policy change, individually or in combination. Comparing those scenarios helps partners estimate consequences such as changes in landfill waste or reliance on imports, and build a strategy for what to trial and who needs to be involved.
